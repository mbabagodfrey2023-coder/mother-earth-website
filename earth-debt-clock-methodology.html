1. Purpose and Scope

The Earth Debt Clock presents humanity's accumulated ecological obligation in quantitative terms — the cost, volume, and rate of environmental degradation caused by human activity since the industrial era. It is designed for scientific literacy, not alarmism. Every metric is classified by its data quality tier and cited to its primary source.

This document describes the calculation method, data source, uncertainty range, and known limitations for every metric on the clock. It is intended for use by researchers, institutional clients, and compliance teams.

3. Hero Counter Calculations

3.1 Carbon Removal Cost (Left Hero Counter)

This is the page's most defensible figure. It is computed live from NOAA's current atmospheric CO₂ reading, not from a static estimate.

excess_ppm = live_CO₂_ppm − 280 ppm (IPCC AR6 pre-industrial baseline) excess_mass = excess_ppm × 7.81 Gt/ppm (IPCC AR6 WG1 Appendix 7A) cost_low = excess_mass × 10⁹ t/Gt × $400/t (IEA Direct Air Capture 2023) cost_high = excess_mass × 10⁹ t/Gt × $1,000/t (Climeworks 2024 commercial price) Example at 432 ppm: excess = 152 ppm → 1,187 Gt CO₂ Range: $475T (low) – $1,187T (high)

Sources:

  • Pre-industrial baseline: IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 2. DOI: 10.1017/9781009157896
  • Mass conversion: IPCC AR6 WG1 Appendix 7A — 1 ppm CO₂ ≈ 7.81 Gt in atmosphere
  • $400/tonne lower bound: IEA Direct Air Capture 2023. iea.org/reports/direct-air-capture-2
  • $1,000/tonne upper bound: Climeworks AG public pricing, 2024. climeworks.com

Limitations: DAC market prices are falling rapidly. $400/tonne represents a blended BECCS/nature-based/DAC scenario; pure BECCS is cheaper, pure DAC is more expensive. This range will require updating annually as the carbon removal market matures.

3.2 Ecosystem Service Loss — 2026 YTD (Right Hero Counter)

This counter ticks from 1 January 2026 at a constant rate. It is a MODEL — an economic estimate from a single academic study, not a measured quantity.

eco_loss_ytd = elapsed_seconds_since_2026_01_01 × (10,000,000,000,000 ÷ 31,536,000) = elapsed_seconds × $317,098/sec

Source: Costanza R. et al. (2014). Changes in the global value of ecosystem services. Global Environmental Change, 26, 152–158. DOI: 10.1016/j.gloenvcha.2014.04.002

Academic controversy — must read before citing this figure: The $10T/year central estimate has been criticised in peer-reviewed literature. Obst et al. (2016) argue that summing marginal values of ecosystem services across their full extent yields double-counting and overstates aggregate welfare impact (DOI: 10.1016/j.jenvman.2016.10.016). Brouwer & van Beukering (2017) note the wide uncertainty bands (literature range $3T–$20T/yr) reflect genuine methodological disagreement, not just measurement noise. The $3T–$20T range disclosed on the clock page reflects this debate. This counter should be read as an illustrative economic framing device, not as an audited financial liability.

11. Live Data Pipeline

Live metrics (LIVE badge) are fetched from MEKE AI's backend API endpoint /api/tipping-points, which in turn queries:

  • CO₂: NOAA GML Mauna Loa weekly CSV — gml.noaa.gov/webdata/ccgg/trends/co2/co2_weekly_mlo.csv
  • CH₄: NOAA GML global mean monthly — gml.noaa.gov/webdata/ccgg/trends/ch4/ch4_mm_gl.txt
  • Temperature: NASA GISTEMP v4 — data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/tabledata_v4/GLB.Ts+dSST.csv
  • Sea ice: NSIDC Sea Ice Index v3 monthly — nsidc.org/data/seaice_index

The pipeline refreshes every 30 minutes via a Cloudflare Worker cron. When an upstream source is unavailable, the most recent successfully fetched value is served with a CACHED label and timestamp indicating when it was last updated.

12. Known Limitations

  • The Costanza 2014 ecosystem service valuation is academically contested. Do not cite the ticking counter as a precise financial figure.
  • All ticking (MODEL) counters apply a constant annual rate to elapsed time. Real-world rates vary by season, region, and year. They are analogous to national debt clocks — useful as order-of-magnitude indicators, not auditable accounts.
  • The 73% vertebrate population decline is an average across monitored populations, not a measure of species extinction probability.
  • Ocean plastic piece counts vary by model assumptions about particle-size thresholds; the published range (82–383 trillion) spans roughly 5×.
  • Arctic sea ice extent is highly seasonal; month-to-month comparisons require seasonal adjustment, which is not applied on the clock.
  • Carbon removal costs are falling year-on-year as DAC technology scales. $400–$1,000/tonne reflects 2024 market prices and should be updated annually.
