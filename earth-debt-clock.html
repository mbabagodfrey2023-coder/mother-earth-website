Note on Costanza et al. 2014 (Ecosystem Service Loss counter): The $10T/year central estimate has been subject to peer-reviewed critique — notably Obst et al. (2016) and Brouwer & van Beukering (2017) — who argue that adding marginal values across all ecosystem services double-counts welfare and overstates the aggregate. The wide $3T–$20T uncertainty range on this page reflects that debate. We present this counter as an illustrative economic frame, not an audited financial metric, and the MODEL badge should be read accordingly. For a fuller treatment see: DOI 10.1016/j.jenvman.2016.10.016.
Scientific transparency statement. This clock separates live measurements from model estimates using visible data quality badges. The Carbon Removal Cost is computed in real time from a live NOAA CO₂ reading and published IEA/Climeworks pricing — it is the most precise figure on the page. The Ecosystem Service Loss counter is a model derived from Costanza et al. 2014, which itself acknowledges a wide uncertainty range; it is shown in amber and labelled "Model" to reflect this. Ticking counters for trees, plastic, and topsoil are rate-based interpolations from peer-reviewed annual figures — the same methodology used by national debt clocks globally. No data on this page is fabricated; all sources are peer-reviewed or from authoritative intergovernmental bodies with DOIs or institutional URLs cited.
